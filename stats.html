The visibility gap 🔍

~300,000 students compete each year — yet participation is concentrated in very few schools. Per MAA's own report, only ~2,129 schools hosted the AMC 8 (2026). Against ~128,000 U.S. schools, that's under 2%. In our Jersey City pilot, just 2 of 64 schools currently show a contest. The room to grow participation is enormous.

Organizations publish the count, not the list of schools — so families can't find them and no one can map growth. Closing that gap is the mission: start with one city, scale with the same code.

The math circle gap 🤝

Contests measure talent; math circles grow it. A circle is a weekly problem-solving group run by parents, teachers, or students, and it is what keeps kids coming back between contests. The national Math Circle Network (American Institute of Mathematics, NSF-supported) counts only hundreds of circles across the entire U.S. In our Jersey City pilot, we know of just 1 math circle, and it is for elementary students only. There is none for middle or high schoolers.

More circles mean more students reaching AMC, AIME, and beyond. Our goal is a parent-, teacher-, or student-run circle in every neighborhood and at every grade level, using AIM's free toolkits and grants. How to start one →
