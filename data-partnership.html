An Open Data Standard for Math Competitions

A partnership proposal for MAA (AMC), MOEMS, MATHCOUNTS, Math League, Math Kangaroo & peers · Math Competition Readiness Hub
The ask in one line: publish a lightweight, standardized, school-level (no student data) feed of participating/host schools — so families can find you, more schools register, and the whole field grows. We've built a working proof and will do the integration work.

The opportunity

Math competitions are one of the most cost-effective on-ramps to STEM — yet no organization publishes queryable participation data, and the lists that do exist are fragmented across PDFs and spreadsheets. The result:

  • Families can't easily find which nearby schools offer a given contest.
  • Schools don't realize they can host, so talent — especially in under-resourced communities — goes undiscovered.
  • Real participation is under-counted, weakening the case for funding and expansion.

Why this helps your organization

  • More registrations. Visibility drives demand — the same reason every school offers the SAT. A public "find a school that offers [your contest]" map pulls new schools in.
  • Lower support burden. A standard feed replaces ad-hoc "who hosts near me?" inquiries.
  • Equity leadership. Surfacing gaps in Title I / rural areas positions you as a leader in STEM access.
  • Zero student-privacy risk. The standard is school-level only — no student names, scores, or PII. COPPA/FERPA-safe by design.

The proposed standard (lightweight — not a heavy API)

It can start as a single published CSV or JSON file, refreshed per cycle. The canonical key is the federal NCES school ID, so data from every organization merges cleanly.

{
  "nces_id":   "340783000142",      // canonical U.S. school key (joins all sources)
  "school":    "Academy I",
  "city":      "Jersey City",
  "state":     "NJ",
  "contest":   "AMC 8",             // AMC 8/10/12, MOEMS, MATHCOUNTS, Math League, Kangaroo
  "role":      "host",              // host | participant | test-center
  "grades":    "6-8",
  "cycle":     "2026-2027"
}
PrincipleWhy
NCES ID as keyClean merge across organizations and with gov school data
School-level onlyNo student PII — privacy-safe, low legal overhead
CSV/JSON, per cycleTrivial to publish; no engineering lift required
Open license + attributionReusable, with clear credit to the source org
No engineers required. You don't need to build software. If you can export your host-school list to a spreadsheet, you can publish this standard: save it as CSV, or drop it in a Google Sheet and use File → Share → Publish to web → CSV. Prefer not to publish at all? Just email us the export each cycle and we'll handle the ingestion.

Proof of concept — one city, already built

We've built a free public registry and piloted it in Jersey City: all 64 schools (public, charter, private, coaching centers) sourced from NCES, with filters by type, grade level, and contest, plus a no-login crowdsourcing form that lets any parent or teacher verify what a school offers or report a new registration.

  • Live pilot: vineetagrwal.github.io/math-dance-pathway/jersey-city.html
  • The same ingestion pipeline that handles 64 schools scales to 130,000+ nationwide — one adapter per data source.

The bigger picture — an AI-driven STEM-opportunity marketplace

A static directory isn't the goal. The real problem is demand generation: today almost no one actively markets math competitions to families, so opportunity — and talent — goes undiscovered. We're building the AI-driven marketing & matching engine that's missing, designed to run as a mission-first non-profit (or sustainable for-profit):

  • A two-sided marketplace. Demand (students, parents, teachers seeking opportunities) meets supply (contests, host schools, test centers) — and the platform generates and routes demand that any single organization can't reach alone.
  • AI-driven matching & outreach. Personalized recommendations ("the right contest for this grade, level, and location"), automated deadline nudges, and targeted outreach to the schools and communities most likely to benefit — especially under-resourced ones.
  • STEM literacy at scale. Collapsing the discovery and access barrier gets more students into rigorous math earlier, strengthening the national STEM pipeline.
  • For your organization: this is the growth/marketing engine you don't run in-house — more registrations, measurable retention, and reach into new communities, at no cost to you. A shared open data standard is the fuel that makes it work.
The national stakes: competitive-math fluency is upstream of advanced STEM and AI talent. Widening access isn't only an equity win — it's a strategic lever for U.S. and allied competitiveness in the AI era, growing the pipeline of problem-solvers a nation needs to lead.

What we're asking for

  1. Publish (or co-define with us) a standardized school-level participation feed as above.
  2. Start with a one-cycle pilot — even a static CSV export is enough to begin.
  3. We handle ingestion, display, and attribution; you get more visibility and participation.
